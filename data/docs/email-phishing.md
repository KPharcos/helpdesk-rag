# Email Security and Reporting Phishing

Phishing emails try to trick you into revealing your password, approving MFA prompts, opening malware or paying fake invoices. Phishing is the most common way attackers get into company systems.

## Warning signs

- Unexpected urgency: "your account will be closed today", "pay this invoice now".
- The sender's address does not match the display name, or uses a look-alike domain such as brightfie1d.example.
- Links whose real address (hover over them) does not match the text.
- Requests for passwords, MFA codes, gift cards or changes to bank details.
- Unexpected attachments, especially .zip, .html, .iso or macro-enabled Office files.

Emails from outside the company are marked with an "[EXTERNAL]" tag in the subject line. Be extra careful with external emails that claim to be from colleagues or IT.

## Reporting a suspicious email

1. Do not click links, open attachments or reply.
2. In Outlook, click the "Report Phishing" button on the ribbon (in the web and mobile apps it is under "Report").
3. The message is removed from your inbox and sent to the security team for analysis.

If you cannot use the button, forward the message as an attachment to security@brightfield.example.

## If you clicked a link or entered your password

Act quickly; minutes matter.

1. Change your password immediately using the self-service portal.
2. Report the incident to security@brightfield.example and call the IT Service Desk 24/7 line.
3. If you opened an attachment, disconnect the laptop from the network (turn off Wi-Fi) but do not switch it off.

You will not be punished for reporting a mistake. Fast reporting lets the security team block the attack before it spreads.

## Mailbox limits and large files

Mailboxes hold 100 GB and individual messages can be up to 25 MB. To share larger files, use a OneDrive sharing link instead of an attachment.
